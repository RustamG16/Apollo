## Environment limitation, stated first

**Pixel screenshots at the 1920×1080 target could not be obtained in this environment.** The
in-app browser pane suspends `requestAnimationFrame` and does not present new frames after
the first paint following a navigation; a `requestAnimationFrame` probe never resolved, and
`scroll-behavior: smooth` — which also depends on it — never completed a programmatic scroll.
Emulating 1920×1080 rendered the page into roughly 215px of the captured surface. Claude in
Chrome, the other browser surface, is not connected in this session.

What was done instead, and what it is worth:

- Everything structural, geometric and chromatic was verified by reading **computed values
  and measured rectangles from the live DOM**, which is stronger evidence than a screenshot
  for contrast and layout and weaker for aesthetic judgement.
- Contrast under the hero type was measured by **rasterising the composited hero to a canvas**
  — the photograph at its real `object-fit`/`object-position`, plus both scrims — and reading
  the brightest pixel beneath each text element. That is a harder test than a token-table check.
- Composition was checked at the pane's own width where it does paint legibly.

**Outstanding: a human pass at 1920×1080 on the target display.** This is listed as a launch
blocker in `09-handoff.md`.

## Pass-one findings and repairs

1. **High · hero unreadable.** The doors opened at 94% cover, slicing the wordmark, eyebrow
   and opening line in half. Repaired: the cover curve was rederived from what each beat's
   content actually needs, and later reduced to a one-shot arrival gesture.
2. **High · wordmark rendered twice.** StrictMode's double-invoke reverted the SplitText split
   without killing its tween, leaving GSAP animating detached character spans. Repaired by
   killing the tween alongside the split.
3. **High · entrances could strand content invisible.** `gsap.from({opacity: 0})` leaves
   content hidden whenever the animation does not complete. Repaired by replacing every
   section entrance with an IntersectionObserver class toggle and a CSS transition, so the
   end state is the stylesheet default whether the transition ran or not. Sections already on
   screen are shown at once rather than hidden and faded back in.
4. **High · the design was timid.** Comparison against a sibling run of this system showed a
   6vw hero against a doctrine calling for 12–18vw, photography boxed inside text columns and
   dimmed to 42%, and a page-wide door mask that subtracted width from every section.
   Repaired: hero to 15.8vw, section display to ~5vw, photography full-bleed at full opacity,
   doors reduced to an arrival gesture. Recorded in full in `DESIGN.md`.
5. **High · contrast failure on the pass beat.** Making the section background follow each
   course photograph put static text tokens on a moving background — measured 3.37:1 for the
   eyebrow and 3.72:1 for a placeholder. Repaired by scoping the measured field to the plate
   panel, which is where the effect is needed anyway. Sweep now clean.
6. **Medium · hero eyebrow over the lit window** at 1.44:1, then 2.32:1. Repaired with a scrim
   that belongs to the type block rather than to the picture, raised above the eyebrow.
7. **Medium · text columns too narrow for the display face** — a 323px column carrying 62px
   type, five characters to a line. Repaired by stacking the early beats.
8. **Medium · the clip was the wrong building.** Its first four seconds show the bistro
   register the audit rejected. Repaired by trimming to the final 3.4s, cropping and grading;
   declared in the asset manifest.
9. **Medium · scroll cue collided with the wordmark.** Repaired by moving it bottom-right.
10. **Medium · section eyebrows slid under the fixed header.** Repaired with 9rem top padding
    and `scroll-margin-top`.
11. **Low · design-system drift**, three findings from the mechanical detector: a 1px focus
    radius against a square-only scale, and two off-ramp font sizes. Two were fixed to tokens;
    the third was a real component step and is now documented as `typography.action`.

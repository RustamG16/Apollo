## The five highest-leverage facts

### 1. Nobody is in this restaurant. (observation)

Twenty-eight of thirty stills contain no person. The two that do show one chef, alone, at a
pass, in a kitchen with no service running. The dining room is photographed laid and empty
in three separate frames. A table is set for two that no one is sitting at. The only other
human being in the entire set is an anonymous figure walking *past* the window from outside,
in the street, not entering.

This is the set's most conspicuous property and no brief asked for it. It is also the fact
the two prior runs on this media did not build on — both told a story about a guest arriving.
The photography does not show a guest arriving. It shows the hour before anyone does.

### 2. The photography is genuinely dark, and it is dark in one specific hue. (measured)

Twenty of thirty stills have a mean luminance below 90/255. Eight sit below 65. Four are
above 55% near-black by pixel count (`Chef_plating_dish` 68%, `SAVRA_table_prepared_for_two`
63%, `Chef_plating_at_pass` 62%, `Threshold_opening_to_stone_table` 57%).

The hue is not neutral. Of the 60 top-two dominant colours across the set, **38 fall within
RGB distance 90 of the brand's Dark Slate Gray `#335C67`.** Auburn appears twice in that
band, hunyadi twice, vanilla three times.

*Inference:* a light page would fight this material continuously. The page ground must be a
slate-cast near-black, and the three warm brand colours are events, not equal partners.

### 3. The palette is not evenly distributed — it is one ambient colour and three punctuations. (measured, and it corrects the initial hypothesis)

The direction as first drafted proposed four equal colour fields advancing on scroll. The
measurement says that is not what the room is. Slate is the ambient truth of the world;
auburn is the lacquer, hunyadi is the lamp and the tile, vanilla is the linen and the spill
of light through a door. They arrive, they are brief, and they are the reason the frame is
worth looking at.

*Consequence for the direction:* the page lives in slate for most of its length, and auburn,
hunyadi and vanilla each get exactly one earned arrival. Four equal quarters would be a
decorative reading of a palette that the photography treats as dramatic.

### 4. One photograph's background is the brand colour, to within three RGB units. (measured)

`Dish_plated_on_dark_slate` is an overhead shot on a flat ground measured at `#365862`,
38% of the frame. Brand slate is `#335C67`. That is a distance of about 8 in RGB — visually
identical at any realistic display gamma.

*Consequence:* that image can be laid onto a CSS field of the same colour with no visible
seam. The plate then appears to be sitting on the page rather than inside a rectangle. This
is the single most useful technical fact in the set, and it is the thing that turns "colour
field" from a decorative idea into a physical one.

Two other overheads (`Salt-baked_beetroot_dish_on_plate`, and the same `Dish_plated_on_dark_slate`)
place the plate hard right with a large empty ground at left — they are composed for
typographic overlay, whether or not that was intended.

### 5. The plate is the constant. (observation)

Nine of the twelve dish photographs use the **same teal-slate glazed ceramic**, and three of
them carry the same embossed maker's mark. Cutlery in `Table_settings_with_linen_runner` is
engraved SAVRA; the kitchen counter in `Chef_plating_at_pass` is engraved SAVRA.

*Consequence:* a menu section does not need to cross-fade between photographs. The plate
genuinely persists across the set, so the plate can persist across the interaction — the
same object, re-dressed. That is continuity in the doctrine's sense (elements transform
between states) rather than a fade, and it is true to the material rather than imposed on it.

## Registers — the set is not one world

The set contains at least three distinguishable art directions. Treating them as one would
produce a page that looks like a stock-photo assembly. Grouping is visible on the contact
sheet.

| Register | Count | What it is | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ROOM** | 6 | The formal lacquered SAVRA: oxblood panelling with brass inlay, mustard tile pilasters, dark slate floor and table tops, black oak, oatmeal linen, smoked-glass lamps. Consistent lighting, consistent architecture. | The spine. The page is built from this. |
| **MATERIAL** | 2 | Close crops: linen over stone over brass over black oak; a place setting with engraved cutlery. | The proof. Used where the page has to argue craft rather than assert it. |
| **HAND** | 3 | One chef, alone, at a pass. Warm task light, deep shadow, tweezers. | The only human evidence. Used once, at the point where the page needs a person. |
| **PLATE** | 12 | Dishes on the same teal ceramic across slate, wood and flat grounds. | The menu, and the Flip continuity device. |
| **PLAN** | 5 | Flat, graphic, colour-field architectural compositions — arches and thresholds rendered as blocks of the four brand colours — plus the palette card itself. Reads as poster, not documentary. | A deliberate interlude where the page names its own palette. Not mixed with ROOM. |
| **OUTLIERS** | 2 | `Restaurant_entrance_view_for_hero` is a different building entirely (a classic French bistro with red-painted doors and a table lamp, not the modern slate-framed SAVRA shopfront). `Recreating_dish_from_asset` is shot in daylight by a window — the only daylight frame in the set. | **Not used.** Both would break the world. |

Two stills are duplicate passes at the same subject (`Salt-baked_beetroot_dish_on_plate` /
`Salt-baked_beetroot_with_labneh`, `Saffron_poached_quince_dessert` /
`Quince_dessert_with_yogurt_and`). Use one of each; the second is a fallback crop.

## Deconstruction — the reference set as a system

**Thesis, ROOM:** a room built from four materials and lit for one occupied table at a time,
photographed at the moment it is ready and still empty.

**Thesis, PLATE:** one plate, many times, always against stone, always with the food as the
only bright object in the frame.

**Thesis, PLAN:** the same architecture flattened into blocks of pure brand colour — the
building as a diagram of its own palette.

**Composition.** ROOM is symmetric and centred, shot at seated eye height, with a strong
frame-within-a-frame in four of six images (a door, a window, a threshold, a proscenium).
PLATE is the opposite: asymmetric, plate hard right or hard low, with 40–60% of the frame
empty ground. The set therefore hands over both a centred, formal register and an
off-centre, editorial one — and the editorial one is where type belongs.

**Typography.** None present in the photography beyond the engraved SAVRA wordmark on the
shopfront lintel, the cutlery and the counter — all set in a wide, light, generously
letterspaced capital serif. That is the only type evidence in the set, and it is a real
brand signal rather than a stylistic preference.

**Colour and material.** Four locked colours, plus three material constants that are not
colours: slate stone (matte, veined), oatmeal linen (visible weave), and brass (a hairline,
never a plane). Lighting is uniformly warm, low, and pooled — every ROOM frame has a
discrete pool of light and a large area of unlit space. There is no ambient fill anywhere in
the set.

**Media system.** Consistent within registers, inconsistent across them. See the table above.

**Interaction grammar.** Not applicable — the references are still images and one clip. The
clip is 8 seconds, 24fps, 1280×720, a slow forward camera move through the dining room, and
**it carries an AAC audio track**.

**Content behaviour.** The set implies a six-course menu through its filenames alone:
flatbread with smoked butter, salt-baked beetroot with labneh, charcoal sea bass with fennel,
lamb backstrap with cherry glaze, slow-cooked lamb shoulder, saffron-poached quince. The
cooking reads as Levantine-Anatolian technique in a formal Viennese room. No prices, no
provenance text, no chef name, no address exists anywhere in the material.

**Responsive inference.** Moot for this run — the user has fixed a single 1920×1080 target.
Worth recording that the set contains only four portrait frames of the ROOM/PLAN registers,
so a future narrow composition would be short of vertical material.

**Risks.**

- *Accessibility.* The locked palette is the main one. Vanilla `#FFF3B0` and hunyadi
  `#E09F3E` are both light; auburn `#9E2A2B` is dark. Only a subset of pairings clears 4.5:1
  and they must be fixed in advance, not composed by eye. Contrast failure on exactly this
  palette is what scored the first Codex run 82/100.
- *Performance.* Sources are small (≤1376px) — payload risk is low, but a 1920-wide layout
  will upscale a 1376px image by 1.4×. Softness is the cost; upscaling is not the fix.
- *Provenance.* Every still carries a four-point generation sparkle in the lower-right
  corner, and the clip has an audio track. Both must be handled in the pipeline, not hidden
  by layout.
- *Trend dependence.* Low. The material is photographic and architectural, not stylistic.
- *Copying risk.* None — the reference set is the client's own supplied media.

## Principle matrix

Five, maximum, per the skill. Each is transformed through the target rather than transferred.

| # | Principle | Evidence | Why it works | Transfer to SAVRA on screen | Do not copy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Light is pooled, never ambient.** | Every ROOM frame has one discrete lit area and a large unlit remainder; no fill light anywhere in the set. | Attention has exactly one place to go, and the darkness is not empty — it is the room. | The page ground is a slate-cast near-black held for most of its length, with light arriving as a bounded event: one warm field, one lit image, one glow. Never a lit page with dark accents. | A "dark theme" — a light layout with the colours inverted. The set is not inverted; it is unlit. |
| 2 | **The frame within the frame.** | Four of six ROOM images look *through* something: a shopfront, a door ajar, a proscenium opening, an arch enfilade. | The viewer is always positioned outside, looking in. Desire is produced by the boundary, not by the interior. | Sections are apertures — the media is revealed through a mask that opens, rather than a rectangle that fades in. The reservation is the moment the aperture stops being a mask. | Literal window-frame chrome, ornamental borders, or a "peek through the keyhole" motif repeated as decoration. |
| 3 | **Slate is ambient; the warm three are events.** | 38 of 60 dominant colours are within RGB-90 of slate; auburn, hunyadi and vanilla appear in that band twice, twice and three times. | The palette's power is that three of its four colours are rare. Spending them evenly spends them. | Slate near-black ground throughout; auburn, hunyadi and vanilla each arrive exactly once, each tied to a specific beat, each earned. | Four equal colour blocks, gradient blends between brand colours, or hunyadi as a general-purpose accent on every interactive element. |
| 4 | **The plate persists; the food changes.** | The same teal ceramic in nine of twelve dish frames, three carrying the same maker's mark. | Continuity is already true of the object, so continuity on screen is documentary rather than an effect. | The menu is one plate that re-dresses itself between courses (GSAP Flip on a shared element), not a gallery that cross-fades. | Flip applied elsewhere because it is available. It earns its place here because the physical object is genuinely the same one. |
| 5 | **The room is legible as a diagram of its own palette.** | The PLAN register renders the same architecture as flat blocks of the four brand colours; the set even ships the palette card. | The brand can state its own system without a paragraph of brand copy. | One quiet interlude where the four colours are named against the flat PLAN compositions. Interlude, not spectacle — the page already has its one hero moment elsewhere. | Turning this into the hero. Two hero moments is the doctrine's explicit refusal. |

## Contradictions between references

1. **Three buildings, one name.** The modern slate-framed SAVRA shopfront, the classic French
   bistro with red doors, and the mid-century colour-block dining room are not the same
   restaurant. Resolved by building only from the ROOM register and dropping
   `Restaurant_entrance_view_for_hero`.
2. **Documentary versus poster.** ROOM/PLATE are photographic with real lighting; PLAN is
   flat, graphic and shadowless. Resolved by giving PLAN its own bounded interlude rather
   than intercutting it.
3. **Night versus day.** One frame (`Recreating_dish_from_asset`) is daylight; everything
   else is after dark. Dropped.
4. **Formal versus casual.** The lacquered symmetric room and the mid-century room with
   bentwood chairs imply different price registers. The formal room wins because the plating,
   the engraved cutlery and the shopfront all agree with it.

## Asset and media implications

- Corner mark on all 30 stills — a ~40px crop from the lower-right clears it at source
  resolution. Must happen in the pipeline; layout must not be asked to hide it.
- Audio on the clip must be stripped; it may never play.
- One derivative tier, sized for the 1920×1080 target, per the user's constraint.
- `Dish_plated_on_dark_slate` should be colour-matched to the CSS slate field deliberately —
  this is a design decision, not an accident, and belongs in the asset manifest.
- 24 of 30 stills are usable. Two are dropped as outliers, two are duplicate passes held as
  fallbacks, and the palette card is a specification rather than a photograph.
- Missing and unobtainable from the media: address, hours, telephone, prices, chef name,
  logo vector, and any image above 1376px. All become launch blockers.

## Keep / transform / avoid

**Keep.** The darkness, at its measured level. Pooled light. The frame-within-a-frame.
The teal ceramic as a recurring object. The engraved wide capital serif as the type signal.
The emptiness — it is the subject, not a deficiency.

**Transform.** The four-colour palette, from a flat swatch card into a sequence of three
earned arrivals over a slate ground. The PLAN register, from an inconsistency into a named
interlude. The dish overheads with their empty grounds, from photographs into type-bearing
surfaces.

**Avoid.** Lightening the photography to suit a light layout. Spending all four colours
evenly. Mixing PLAN with ROOM. Using the French-bistro frame. Any second hero moment.
Any motion that delays reading.

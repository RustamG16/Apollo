## One-project setup, generated references, and continuity workflow

This guide uses your visible Google Flow project interface. It needs only the already-saved characters `@Apollo` and `@Rustam`; Flow creates every other visual reference internally.

## What not to add

Do not add reference photos, Pinterest boards, a real-room photograph, a hand video, a storyboard, theatre shots, lighting stills, or external mood boards. Those were removed because they compete with the requested single, self-generated continuity system.

## Project-wide Agent Instructions

Open **Agent Instructions** from the lower-right of the Flow Agent panel, as in your screenshot. Add each instruction with its toggle on. Leave the **Reference** box empty: character assets and generated predecessor frames are supplied in the session when needed.

### 1. Identity and continuity

Title: `Apollo identity + continuity`

```
Use @Apollo as the immutable identity master: preserve his face, age, hair, body proportions and recognizable character design in every output. Use @Rustam as the immutable creator identity master only where he or his arm/hand appears. After GEN_WORLD_LOCK is approved, every later image or clip must derive from the prior approved generated asset and preserve its theatre architecture, curtain, orchestra, costumes, palette, lighting, lens, camera height, direction and momentum. No text-only fresh scene generation, morphing, dissolves, portals, teleports or redesigns at joins.
```

### 2. Cinema world

Title: `Neo-Athenian cinema world`

```
Create photoreal live-action cinema: a dark Neo-Athenian theatre with black/dark stone, selective marble columns and statues, bronze, deep-red velvet curtains, real rigging, warm tungsten practical lighting and only thin cyan structural accents. Use natural materials, readable blacks, subtle 35mm grain, gentle halation, clean lens rendering and cinematic depth of field. Avoid neon cities, particles, fog, hologram clutter, fantasy magic, game-engine surfaces, plastic skin and excessive bloom.
```

### 3. Orchestra and design language

Title: `Orchestra realism + design manifestation`

```
The orchestra is a real, professional ensemble in refined dark concert clothing. Maintain believable anatomy, instrument construction, playing posture, stage spacing and stable instrument identity. When music creates design, resolve grids, type hierarchy, modular components, layout frames and responsive systems physically into the architecture. Never use generic floating dashboard holograms.
```

### 4. Camera and ending geometry

Title: `Camera path + physical ending`

```
Camera movement is smooth, curved and deliberate like a stabilized cinema dolly, never handheld, racing FPV or a turntable. Follow: backstage push, curtain opening with Apollo already waiting, graceful orbit, orchestra aisle, one readable instrument swap, architectural design manifestation, move into one lower theatre corner, face the diagonally opposite upper corner, upward tilt, open boundary into a larger generated studio, then @Rustam's hand enters from that opposite upper corner and physically grips/pulls the camera. No conventional roof, literal box, portal, dissolve or explosion.
```

### 5. Text-safe video

Title: `Text-safe hero framing`

```
Do not generate typography or explanatory text in the footage. During the Apollo, agents, skills and design-DNA beats, put the main subject on one third and keep the opposite third darker, quieter and clear for HTML overlay. From the upward reveal onward, use image-only composition with no text-safe requirement.
```

## Operating sequence

1. In a new Agent session, mention `@Apollo` and `@Rustam`, then paste `02_APOLLO_GOOGLE_FLOW_MASTER_BRIEF.txt`.
2. Let the Agent propose only `GEN_WORLD_LOCK`; inspect it and approve it only if it establishes Apollo, the theatre, orchestra, lighting and lens correctly.
3. Generate one named still at a time. When prompting a new still, attach/mention the immediately previous approved still and say: “Use this as the continuity source. Continue the camera from its exact final position; change only the requested next beat.”
4. Once all eight frames are approved, generate clips in the listed order. Use the prior clip’s clean saved final frame as the start reference for the next clip.
5. Assemble and trim only on matching end/start frames. A bad local result is regenerated from its fixed predecessor; never replace the World Lock to solve a later-shot problem.

## Fast repair prompts

- Identity drift: `Keep the exact composition and action. Lock @Apollo to the approved predecessor; do not change face, hair, age, body or wardrobe identity.`
- Scene morph: `Use the attached approved predecessor as the unmodified continuity source. Preserve its architecture, lighting, palette, lens and camera direction exactly; continue only the requested action.`
- Bad instruments: `Preserve exact real-world instrument type, construction and hand anatomy throughout. No morphing or substitution.`
- Box reveal: `Remove all container/box cues. The theatre remains a full-scale believable world; only the upward diagonal tilt reveals an open architectural boundary into the larger generated studio.`
- Bad grab: `Continue until @Rustam's anatomically correct fingers physically wrap around the lens, the camera reacts on contact, and the viewpoint is pulled upward into the studio.`

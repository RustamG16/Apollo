# Reference Deconstruction

Analyze references as systems, not collections of attractive details. Preserve the source/URL and distinguish direct observation from interpretation.

## Inspect each reference

Record:

1. **Thesis** — the single experience idea holding the work together.
2. **Composition** — grid, density, whitespace, alignment, cropping, and focal path.
3. **Typography** — scale contrast, roles, width, case, rhythm, and editorial behavior.
4. **Color and material** — palette roles, contrast, texture, lighting, depth, and surface language.
5. **Media system** — subject, framing, art direction, consistency, and relationship to content.
6. **Interaction grammar** — entry, hover, scroll, transitions, feedback, and pacing.
7. **Content behavior** — narrative order, voice, proof, and call-to-action treatment.
8. **Responsive inference** — what likely collapses, reorders, or becomes simpler.
9. **Risks** — accessibility, performance, production cost, trend dependence, and copying risk.

## Synthesize principles

Create a short matrix:

| Principle | Evidence | Why it works | Transfer to target brand | Do not copy |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Extract no more than five high-leverage principles. Transform them through the target’s audience, content, assets, and constraints. Reject details that depend on another brand’s identity or unavailable production quality.

## Output

Return:

- a one-sentence visual thesis for each reference;
- the principle matrix;
- contradictions between references;
- asset/media implications;
- motion/WebGL candidates, clearly marked as candidates rather than requirements;
- a short “keep / transform / avoid” summary.

Do not create redesign concepts in this phase. Do not infer hidden implementation details as fact.
